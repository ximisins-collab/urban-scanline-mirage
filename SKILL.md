# Urban Scanline Mirage

Rebuild a real urban scene as an ordered spatial memory. The result is not a photograph with stripes: the **protected action unit stays photographic while every other region is recomposed from source-derived rectilinear fields with varied horizontal chromatic strata**.

**REQUIRED SUB-SKILL:** Use `imagegen` for the image edit and inspect the result before delivery.

## Protected Action Unit

Protect the focal person or small focal group together with:

- objects they physically hold, touch, operate, sit on, or visibly depend on;
- necessary connections such as an umbrella handle, kite string, bicycle contact, ladder, tool, or carried object;
- immediate ground contact and the real grounding shadow.

Keep this unit crisp, photographic, anatomically unchanged, correctly scaled, and in the source position. Do not enlarge, restyle, duplicate, scanline, or relight it. A nearby umbrella, vehicle, person, or prop is not protected unless the physical connection is visually clear.

## Scene Grammar

| Source role | Reconstructed treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Sky, ground, water, or one large wall | One spacious dominant-hue field with low-contrast horizontal strata that vary subtly by zone. Fully stylized; never left photographic. |
| Major building or spatial mass | One or a few oversized axis-aligned rectangles or bars. Give each major object one dominant source colour rather than many internal fragments. |
| Secondary type: vehicle, bin, lamp, umbrella, planter, laundry, tree, sign | A minimal rectangular scanline marker in approximately the source area. Preserve position, colour, and visual weight—not natural contour, parts, or surface detail. |
| Repeated type | Keep only the smallest representative sample that preserves the positional rhythm; absorb the rest into its parent field. |
| Spatial anchor | Retain a few narrow façade edges, columns, doorways, poles, or ladders as vertical rectangles when needed for legibility. |
| Accent colour | Use only source-derived accents. Let a few warm, red, yellow, cobalt, cyan, or green bars organise the composition. |

Every non-protected mark is a front-facing square, rectangle, horizontal bar, or narrow vertical anchor. Depth comes from overlap, cropping, scale, and position—not bevels, side faces, gradients, perspective extrusion, or realistic environmental shadows.

## Adaptive Density

Match the field density to the source without copying its detail:

- **Open scene:** let sky, pavement, water, or wall dominate as calm low-information fields; compress architecture into a narrow or sparse rhythm.
- **Ordinary street:** use a small hierarchy of large façade and ground fields plus sparse secondary markers and vertical anchors.
- **Dense market, alley, or façade:** allow more overlapping rectangles, but make the result substantially calmer than the source. Repeated objects become representative markers, not a complete inventory.

Complexity is a reason to compress more. Do not use a fixed tile count or equal scanline density across the frame.

## Chromatic Material

The scanline treatment is source-derived material, not a global CRT or CSS overlay.

- Give every field one dominant sampled hue plus nearby lighter/darker, slightly warmer/cooler, subtly impure shades.
- Mix hairline strata, medium streaks, and occasional wider light/dark bands. Vary pitch, opacity, merging, fading, and interruptions between zones.
- Keep sky and open ground low-contrast and spacious; use richer tonal layering for dense façades, foliage, concrete, or market masses. Accents stay few and source-derived.
- Keep the field boundary crisp and rectilinear, but let its interior retain layered optical texture. Do not substitute flat vector fills, random grain, paper texture, or brushwork.

## Build the Image

1. Lock the source orientation, aspect ratio, viewpoint, horizon, protected action unit, and broad light/dark organisation.
2. Map only the broadest semantic zones: protected unit, sky, ground, major left/right or near/far masses, distant opening, and meaningful secondary types.
3. Assign each major zone one dominant colour sampled from the source. Keep natural source colour relationships; do not import the palette of a style reference.
4. Replace every non-protected zone with oversized rectilinear fields. Fill each field with uneven source-derived horizontal chromatic strata that read as material, not a global overlay.
5. Reduce secondary types to rectangular markers. Remove wheels, mirrors, handles, ribs, leaves, fixtures, readable object silhouettes, and other literal components.
6. Reintroduce only the vertical anchors needed to recognize the place. Keep genuine blank or low-information areas.
7. Generate, inspect at full-frame scale, and revise only the failing layer.

## Sky and Ground Consistency

Sky and ground must reach the same **degree of transformation** as the buildings while using a calmer **density of information**.

- A sky is usually one calm source-blue, grey, or dusk field with soft low-contrast strata—not realistic clouds, an untouched photographic opening, or stacked high-contrast slabs.
- Pavement and wet roads become broad source-colour scanline planes with the same layered material language as the façades. Retain the direction and broad light path, but compress puddles, manholes, texture, and continuous mirror reflections into restrained bands or small rectangular hints.
- Do not leave a broad photographic corridor around the person. Preserve only the immediate contact zone required for grounding.

## Prompt Recipe

> Preserve the focal person and every object physically connected to their action as one crisp photographic unit, including immediate ground contact and true shadow. Rebuild every other part of the same scene from a few oversized source-colour squares, rectangles, and bars. Build each field from its dominant source hue plus nearby lighter/darker, slightly warmer/cooler, subtly impure horizontal chromatic strata; mix hairline lines, medium streaks, and occasional wider bands with uneven pitch, opacity, merging, fading, and interruptions. Keep sky and open ground calm and low-contrast, and give dense architectural or foliage fields richer layered texture. Reduce vehicles, lamps, bins, umbrellas, planters, signs, and repeated objects to minimal rectangular markers in their source areas. Retain only sparse vertical anchors and broad colour relationships. Keep the original frame, scene identity, and source palette. Flat, quiet, precise urban memory; no literal background objects, uniform CRT overlay, or flat vector fill.

## Failure Corrections

| Symptom | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Looks like a photograph under scanlines | Replace sky, ground, and secondary objects structurally; do not increase overlay density. |
| Vehicles, umbrellas, bins, lamps, or planters are still obvious objects | Remove complete contours and components; retain only a rectangular colour-and-position marker. |
| Sky or road feels weaker than the buildings | Keep it as one calm large field, but add fine same-language sampling throughout; do not chop it into broad staircase bands. |
| Result becomes noisy or mosaic-like | Merge fragments into fewer, larger one-colour fields and subsample repeated types. |
| Lines are too clean, evenly spaced, or CSS-like | Keep the large rectilinear geometry, then vary each field's source-derived hue shifts, pitch, opacity, streak width, merging, and interruptions. Do not add generic noise or texture. |
| Scene identity disappears | Restore horizon, dominant mass positions, source colour rhythm, distant opening, and a few vertical anchors—not object detail. |
| Protected subject distorts | Expand the clean protection zone around the action unit and regenerate that layer. |

## Quality Gate

Deliver only when all are true:

1. The protected action unit is the only natural photographic contour and remains anatomically and spatially unchanged.
2. The original place is recognizable from massing, horizon, colour rhythm, and sparse anchors rather than literal background objects.
3. Sky, ground, buildings, and secondary zones are all fully reconstructed; no photographic background corridor remains.
4. Each major zone reads as one spacious dominant-colour field with varied source-derived horizontal chromatic material, not uniform striping, flat fill, or multicolour internal patchwork.
5. Secondary types remain perceptible only through approximate position, colour, scale, and rhythm.
6. All abstracted shapes are flat and rectilinear; no curves, rounded object cut-outs, 3D faces, glossy depth, or realistic environmental modelling remain.
7. The palette, light/dark balance, orientation, aspect ratio, and calm gallery-like mood still come from the source.

Avoid uniform CRT scanlines, flat vector fills, random pixel noise, tiny mosaics, pixel sorting, RGB splitting, warped smears, wavy stripes, paper/canvas texture, speckles, brush strokes, neon cyberpunk, holograms, chrome, sci-fi additions, text, logos, and watermarks.
